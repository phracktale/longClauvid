## Lettre sur l'antigène par sexe

Objet : Antigen detection by sex in your multicohort study: one aggregate table

```text
Dear Dr Swank and Dr Walt,

I maintain longClauvid, an open, source-traceable registry of hypotheses on long COVID and ME/CFS (https://github.com/phracktale/longClauvid). I am an independent software developer, not a clinician or a researcher, and part of the registry was drafted with an AI assistant; every claim in it links to its source.

Two hypotheses in the registry disagree on why long COVID affects more women than men: persistent antigen, or hormonal modulation of immunity. Your multicohort study measured plasma antigen in 706 people and adjusted for sex, but I could not find antigen positivity by sex. In the LIINC analysis, antigen was not more frequent in women (prevalence ratio, male versus female, 1.68; 95% CI 0.93 to 3.04).

My question: could you share, or point me to, one aggregate table: the number of participants tested and the number antigen-positive, by sex? If menopausal or hormonal status was recorded, the same split would help.

I am not asking for participant-level data. The analysis plan, with the decision rule written before seeing these numbers, is public (in French): https://github.com/phracktale/longClauvid/blob/main/analyses/lot-1/PLAN.md

If this is already published, a pointer to it would fully answer my question.

Thank you for your work on this illness.

[name]
```

## Lettre sur le croisement de la signature interféron et de l'antigène

Objet : Do your inflammatory PASC subgroups overlap with the antigen-typed participants?

```text
Dear Dr Talla and Dr Torgerson,

I maintain longClauvid, an open, source-traceable registry of hypotheses on long COVID and ME/CFS (https://github.com/phracktale/longClauvid). I am an independent software developer, not a clinician or a researcher, and part of the registry was drafted with an AI assistant; every claim in it links to its source.

Two hypotheses in the registry make opposite predictions. If an interferon loop sustains itself, the interferon signature should be as frequent in people without detectable antigen. If persistent antigen feeds it, the signature should concentrate in antigen carriers. I found no study that measured both in the same people.

Your 2023 paper describes PASC subgroups with type I or type II interferon-driven inflammation. The 2024 multicohort study of circulating antigen, which several of you co-authored, typed 706 people for plasma antigen.

My questions:

1. Do the two studies share participants?
2. If so, could one aggregate table be shared: inflammatory subgroup (type I interferon, type II interferon, none) by antigen detected or not?

I am not asking for participant-level data. The analysis plan, with the decision rule written before seeing these numbers, is public (in French): https://github.com/phracktale/longClauvid/blob/main/analyses/lot-1/PLAN.md

If this is already published, a pointer to it would fully answer my question.

Thank you for your work on this illness.

[name]
```
